# Learner evaluation — protocol, not claimed results

Status: not conducted. Automated browser and logic tests do not establish learning efficacy, enjoyment or enterprise approval. No learner responses or observations have been fabricated.

## Pilot cohort

Ask the training owner to recruit 6–8 consenting adult employees, with a mix of client-facing and delivery experience. Include keyboard-only and screen-reader users in accessibility evaluation. This is a development exercise, not an employee performance assessment. Do not collect client-confidential information.

Use the published build or supplied offline package. Record its version and archive checksum. Test on the actual managed browser and LMS intended for rollout; the included fake SCORM API is a protocol test, not certification of an organisation's LMS.

## Session

1. Give only the instruction: “Open the game and lead the engagement.” Observe whether the learner can explain their role, current chapter and next step without assistance.
2. Record time to the first meaningful business commitment. Target: within three minutes, without rushing reading.
3. At the first investigation, ask what choosing two items means. Check that they distinguish spending discovery capacity from merely opening their account file.
4. Ask them to inspect the brief while drafting a reply, return, close the game and resume. Observe whether the unchanged draft and restored screen are understood.
5. At a consequence, ask “What changed, and why?” Record their own explanation rather than prompting with the intended lesson.
6. Continue to an authored ending. Early loss and walk-away are legitimate completed scenarios. Do not force everyone to take the longest path.
7. Ask the learner to identify an earlier commitment that affected a later moment. Use their actual decision record and causal links.
8. Invite a what-if replay with a different starting advantage or discovery choice. Never present an “optimal path” as the only acceptable workplace behaviour.
9. Ask which action they would use in a real engagement and what evidence they would need before acting.

## Measures and release decision

Capture task success, requests for help, lost-place incidents, unintentional commits, reading fatigue and the learner's own explanation of a consequence. Ask engagement and clarity ratings separately, with an open question about the least convincing moment.

Proposed acceptance targets: every participant can identify chapter and next action; no unrecoverable progress loss or keyboard trap; at least 80% can explain a real earlier-to-later consequence unaided; no recurring confusion between earned evidence and available options. Treat these as proposed pilot thresholds, not validated results.

Record failures by screen/node ID, viewport, browser and run code. A run code reveals choices, not a person's name, but should still be handled as training-session data under the organisation's policy. Obtain consent before recording screens or audio.
